Dark variants of `mode: 'auto'` tone-delta colors (`tone: '+N'` / `'-N'`) now step evenly. A dark delta used to start from the base's light tone, which carries the light window's lift — or, on a contrast-solved base or a mix, sits wherever light put it — so on a tone-50 base `'+4'` came out −5.6 in dark and `'-4'` +1.3, and a `'-4'` hover on an AAA button stepped darker in dark as well. It now starts from where the base sits on the authored scale: its authored tone, or, for a `fixed` or `static` base, an `auto` contrast-solved base, a mix, or an `auto` `'max'` / `'min'` on a base, its dark tone read back through the dark window. A dark delta then lands where the same authored tone would on every base — along chains, for `from` colors, and for `glaze.color()` links (including a base with its own `lightTone`; a base with its own `darkTone` is read back through the link's dark window, so a `'+0'` link lands on it). At the reference dark `eps` every dark step is the dark window's scale times the delta (about 0.87× at the defaults: `'+4'` → −3.5, `'-4'` → +3.5) and every high-contrast dark step is exact, under any light `eps`; a custom dark `eps` reshapes the dark window's curve, so dark and high-contrast dark steps follow that curve instead of a fixed scale. On a `fixed` or `static` base the `auto` dependent still inverts on its own, now mirrored around wherever the base sits: 2.x reflected it across the whole scale, so an `auto` `'+30'` label on a `fixed` tone-52 fill landed 31.5 below it in dark (now 26.0), and a `'-4'` hover on a `fixed` delta riding an `auto` surface landed 22 tones above it (now +3.5). The flip is still decided on the light tone, so near the ends of the scale a step that fits in light can run off the authored scale in dark and stop at the dark window's edge: a negative delta on an `auto` base authored below |N| stops at the top (`'-4'` on tone 2 steps about +1.7 in dark, on tone 0 not at all), and a delta on a base anchored at its own dark tone can stop at either end, in high contrast too, as can a high-contrast step under a custom light `eps`. A base sitting past the dark window — pushed there by a contrast floor or an extreme, or placed there as a `fixed` or `static` color — is stepped from where it sits: a step back toward the window moves by the full scaled delta, and one pointing further out stops at the base instead of snapping back to the window's edge. Dark variants of these colors shift — under the default light window up to about 4 tones on low bases; more on a `fixed`, `static`, contrast-solved, mixed, or extreme base, on one past the window, or under a custom light window or `eps`, since the 2.x error grew with the window's lift (`lightTone: [15, 100]`: about 4 tones on a tone-50 base, up to 8 at the bottom) — along with anything solved, mixed, or lit from them; a contrast-solved delta whose 2.x dark step crossed its base can now land on the other side of it, mirroring light (`{ base: 'fill', tone: '-1', contrast: 3 }` on a tone-50 fill: 16.2 → 89.0 in dark); see "3.0 — even dark steps for tone deltas" in `docs/migration.md`. High-contrast variants change only for deltas on contrast-solved, mixed, extreme, `fixed`, or `static` bases, or under a custom light `eps`; `contrastLevel: 100` still equals the high-contrast tier. Light variants change only through the tie below.

A contrast-solved color whose preferred tone lands within float noise (1e-9 tones) of its base's — a `'+0'` / `'-0'` delta, or an absolute tone equal to the base's — no longer takes a side from that noise, in any scheme, light included: the solver's own tie rule picks the side, so such a color can land on the other side of its base, or move by a few thousandths of a tone where it keeps its side. Under a manual `contrastLevel`, a probe that lands on its base likewise pins no side.
