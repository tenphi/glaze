Dark variants of `mode: 'auto'` tone-delta colors (`tone: '+N'` / `'-N'`) now step evenly. A dark delta used to start from the base's light tone, which carries the light window's lift — or, on a contrast-solved base or a mix, sits wherever light put it — so on a tone-50 base `'+4'` came out −5.6 in dark and `'-4'` +1.3, and a `'-4'` hover on an AAA button stepped darker in dark as well. It now starts from where the base sits on the authored scale: its authored tone, or, for a contrast-solved base, a mix, or a `'max'` / `'min'` on a base, its dark tone read back through the dark window. A dark delta then lands where the same authored tone would on every base that inverts in dark — along chains, for `from` colors, and for `glaze.color()` links (including a base with its own `lightTone`; a base with its own `darkTone` is read back through the link's dark window, so a `'+0'` link lands on it). At the reference dark `eps` every dark step is the dark window's scale times the delta (about 0.87× at the defaults: `'+4'` → −3.5, `'-4'` → +3.5) and every high-contrast dark step is exact, a custom light `eps` included; a custom dark `eps` reshapes the dark window's curve, so dark and high-contrast dark steps follow that curve instead of a fixed scale. On a `fixed` or `static` base the inversion still follows the dependent's own mode. The flip is still decided on the light tone, so a negative delta on a base authored below |N| stops at the dark window's top (`'-4'` on tone 2 steps about +1.7 in dark, on tone 0 not at all). A base that a contrast floor or an extreme pushed past the dark window is stepped from where it sits: a step back toward the window moves by the full scaled delta, and one pointing further out stops at the base instead of snapping back to the window's edge. Dark variants of these colors shift — up to about 4 tones on low bases, more on a contrast-solved base or one past the window — along with anything solved, mixed, or lit from them. High-contrast variants change only for deltas on contrast-solved, mixed, or extreme bases, or under a custom light `eps`; `contrastLevel: 100` still equals the high-contrast tier. Light variants change only through the tie below.

A contrast-solved color whose preferred tone lands within float noise (1e-9 tones) of its base's — a `'+0'` / `'-0'` delta, or an absolute tone equal to the base's — no longer takes a side from that noise, in any scheme, light included: the solver's own tie rule picks the side, so such a color can land on the other side of its base, or move by a few thousandths of a tone where it keeps its side. Under a manual `contrastLevel`, a probe that lands on its base likewise pins no side.
